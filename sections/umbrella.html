The Umbrella Hall

A Ground Where Coins Are Born

Provadia is not one coin. It is a town with an umbrella over it — and under that umbrella, a Bricklayer's own coin can be born.

Past the square, the town keeps a street of houses not yet built. This hall is that street's registry: the place where the family will live. Every founder who raises a house here carries the town's name into the world with him — an ecosystem of ecosystems, one covenant over all of it.

The Umbrella Charter

The gate is citizenship — Hold the coin, and sign the town covenant. Holding is the proof of belief. It is never a fee paid to the town.

The town builds free — The crew builds thy coin and its starter home under the town's roof, and charges thee nothing for the building. Thy creation costs are thine own: the platform's and the chain's charges, paid from thine own wallet, as Provadia's founder paid his. The town never fronts, covers, or touches a founder's money — and thy wallet and thy signature stay thine alone.

The tithe — A coin born here shares the creator fees its own trading earns: 80 to its founder, 10 to the store that built it, 10 to the town's works. The split is written into the covenant before the coin is born, and every payment is receipted in the public record. The town's tenth funds town works only. Holders of any house's coin are promised nothing.

The law travels with the mark — Every house signs the same covenant before it is born: the honesty sentence, no promises, no yield-bait, receipts for claims. A coin that will not sign does not get the umbrella.

The strike — A house that breaks the covenant is struck from this registry, publicly, with the receipt shown. The umbrella shelters honest houses only.

The Registry of Houses

No houses yet — the first will be receipted here.

The plaques stand ready and blank. Nothing is written on them that has not happened: no sample houses, no borrowed names, no counts of the hopeful. When the first house is born under the umbrella, its name, its founder, and its covenant enter this book — with receipts.

The Founder's Door

This door is a knock, not a signup. The hall opens when the town's charter is forged — the law of the umbrella is being written now, and the town announces nothing before its design survives. There is no form here, no date promised, and no queue to join.

When the door opens, the way in is the charter above: hold the coin · sign the covenant · thy wallet, thy costs. Until then, a knock is heard at the Support Desk — support@provadiacoin.com — and answered by a person of the town.

The umbrella is not an exchange, not a fund, and not a promise that any house's coin is worth anything. The town lists; it does not bless.
